Feature Spec

Operating Partner

A hire-on-demand, per-endeavor startup-management expert that consults with the founder, authors and owns the living plan, tracks it autonomously against reality, and pushes the company forward — showing up in Slack and Drive like a real employee.

Draft Author: Nitay 2026-07-11

1Summary

Pidgin is ready to run itself, and the first thing a founder needs is a management layer. The Executive Assistant already exists; what's missing is the seasoned operator who helps direct near-term decisions and keeps a living plan honest. The Operating Partner is a new bundled recipe for exactly that: an advisory C-level partner with real startup-management expertise, hired on demand, one per endeavor. It consults on decisions, authors and owns the plan and the priorities, tracks it, and has a bias toward pushing the company forward — while the EA owns the logistics stream. Its plan lives as the operator's own spreadsheet in Drive (source of truth), backed by the task backlog for teeth. It self-runs on the autonomy layer: quiet daily change-gated tracking, a weekly digest, and exception pings — treating the plan as agile and proposing revisions when reality drifts. It cannot command anyone; it advises with conviction, and hands work to the EA as a peer request in a channel the owner can see. It talks to the owner in Slack/email like an employee — the in-pidgin DM is a diagnostic path only.

2Scope

In scope

  • A new bundled operating-partner recipe: hire-on-demand, non-singleton, one per endeavor.
  • An expert, opinionated persona — startup-management fluency and a forward-pushing bias, not a neutral facilitator.
  • Advisory posture across strategy and tactics — consults, authors, and owns the plan and priorities; delegate blocked.
  • Plan artifact = the operator's own spreadsheet in Drive (source of truth), backed by the task backlog.
  • Operator-configurable plan format — the recipe adopts the operator's existing template, not a fixed layout.
  • Self-running autonomy: daily change-gated tracking, a weekly digest (supervisor report), and exception pings.
  • Agile plan stewardship: proactively proposes plan revisions when reality drifts.
  • A two-tier write model scoped to its stewarded sheet: tracking cells ungated, canon staged for approval; careful-guest mode on any other document.
  • Real-employee interface — Slack/email; peer hand-offs to the EA in a human-visible channel.
  • Hire-time capture of: display name (a person's first name), operator-set role title, endeavor scope, and the plan-sheet it stewards.
  • The three platform enablers, delivered as part of this plan — PID-15 (Sheets read/write), PID-16 (configurable cadence), PID-17 (template-declared fields + HR coach).

Out of scope

  • Singleton enforcement — deliberately none; "one Chief of Staff" is operator judgment, not a guardrail.
  • Multi-level agent-to-agent reporting (an Operating Partner reporting up to an org-wide CoS) — reports go to the human owner for now.
  • The Executive Assistant recipe — already exists and unchanged.
  • New connectors — Slack and Drive already exist; this builds no new integration.
  • Commanding other agents or owning the logistics stream (calendar, inbox, errands) — that stays with the EA.
  • Giving financial or investment advice — it maintains the owner's own P&L section as data; it is not an advisor of record.

3Why this exists

The trigger is dogfooding: using Pidgin to run Pidgin. Starting a company, the founder needs management before anything else — a PA, and a seasoned operator to help direct the immediate decisions and plan the near future while keeping the plan on track. The first half already ships: the Executive Assistant recipe covers inbox triage, calendar, reminders, and delegation. The second half has no home. Nothing in recipes/ covers consulting-plus-planning-plus-tracking; the closest, marketing-campaign-manager, is domain-specific.

In a real startup this role is the founder's right hand — an experienced operator who consults on what matters, writes the plan, tracks it, and pushes the company forward. It sits below a Chief of Staff in rank and above an administrative coordinator in scope: it plans with the founder and keeps them updated, without directing the org top-down. Pidgin has the primitives — the autonomy layer, the task backlog, the change-gated wakeup, the Drive connector, and the owner-approval gate — but no recipe that composes them into this role, and a few capability gaps underneath it that this plan closes.

4How it works

4.1 Role, altitude & expertise

The Operating Partner is an advisory C-level partner with startup-management expertise, scoped to a single endeavor (a venture or initiative; for the top-level hire, the whole company). It is not a neutral scribe: it brings a point of view — prioritization instinct, stage-awareness, "here's what matters now and why" — and a bias toward pushing the company forward. It spans strategy and tactics: it helps think through the roadmap and the next concrete moves. But its authority is influence, not command — it advises with conviction and it never directs people. It is not a singleton: several can run in parallel, one per endeavor, each owning its own plan. Naming a hire "Chief of Staff" and scoping it org-wide makes it behave as the singleton CoS — but that's convention, set by the operator, not enforced in code.

4.2 The plan artifact

The plan has a durable home: the operator's own spreadsheet in Drive is the source of truth (for visibility and posterity), and the task backlog gives it teeth — actionable lines mirrored to tracked tasks with real open/in_progress/blocked status. The plan's format is operator-configurable: there is no single right layout, so the recipe adopts the operator's existing template rather than imposing one. For the first target operator that's an Excel/Sheets workbook with tabs like P&L, short-term objective, product vision, hiring, and major blockers. Bootstrap is therefore ingest, not blank-slate interview: on first run it reads Enablement's company context, then reads the existing sheet, understands its sections, and works with it — asking the owner to fill gaps rather than inventing structure. This also cleanly separates it from Enablement: Enablement owns what the company is; the Operating Partner owns what we're doing next.

4.3 Rhythm & autonomy

The Operating Partner runs itself on the autonomy layer. On activation its bootstrap wake reads company context, reads its endeavor scope, ingests its plan sheet, opens its mission as a task, and arms its own schedules. It runs on three separate clocks:

  • Daily tracking wake (change-gated) — reconciles plan vs. reality; if nothing material moved it goes back to sleep silently. Its default cadence is daily, not the 30-minute work heartbeat — a strategic partner thrashing every half hour is noise (this default is why PID-16 exists).
  • Weekly digest — fires on schedule regardless (the standing "you'll hear from me Friday" contract); it is the Operating Partner's supervisor report. In a quiet week it's a one-liner: "no material change, plan on track."
  • Exception ping — fires immediately when a signal crosses a severity line (a deadline about to slip, a milestone blocked).

It treats the plan as agile: when reality drifts or new information lands, it proactively drafts a proposed plan revision (staged as a canon-cell change for owner approval — see 4.5) rather than clinging to a stale plan or silently rewriting it.

4.4 What counts as "changed" — the daily wake's gate

The daily wake does real work (and may ping the owner) only if, since its last real run, one of these fired. This maps onto the change-gated wakeup probe:

#SignalNotes
1Backlog deltaA tracked task under its endeavor flipped to blocked/done/at-risk, or a new task appeared.
2Someone edited its plan sheetAnother actor changed the sheet — especially a canon cell (objective, priority, P&L, vision). The owner's edits pull it in to reconcile.
3Deadline-state transitionEdge-triggered: fires once when an item crosses on-track → approaching → overdue, deduped per item. Sitting overdue is not a daily re-trigger — it lives in the weekly digest's at-risk section.
4Escalation / report on its endeavorAn escalation or report touching its endeavor landed.
5Explicit "stop skipping"A human poke ("run a cycle," "wake up") forces a full wake regardless of the gate — the on-demand escape hatch.

General Slack chatter is not a trigger by itself. It reads channels for context when it wakes for a real signal, but a busy channel does not wake it — otherwise the 30-minute thrash we designed away comes back through the side door.

4.5 The write model — two tiers, scoped by ownership

"Tracks on its own" and "every mutation is owner-approved" collide, so writes split by what part of the sheet — and the privilege is scoped to the one artifact it stewards:

  • Tracking cells → ungated. The columns/sections it owns as the tracker — status, last-updated dates, an observed-blockers log, backlog-synced state. It writes these freely; it's mirroring reality, which is the whole point of tracking on its own.
  • Canon cells → staged for approval. The owner's decisions — objective, priorities/ordering, product vision, hiring plan, P&L numbers. Changes here are batched into one approval ("here's my proposed plan revision — approve?"), not per-cell, reusing the batch-approval flow.
  • Any other document → careful guest. On files it doesn't steward, it stages everything for approval, keeps a minimal footprint, never bulk-edits, and when in doubt doesn't touch. The ungated privilege applies to only the plan sheet it was pointed at during hire.

This deliberately bends the "one uniform write gate" principle from the Enablement design (all shared-storage writes owner-approved). It's a conscious trade to kill approval fatigue, defensible because tracking cells are factual mirrors, not authored content. Broken on purpose, not by accident.

4.6 Coordination & hand-offs

Because delegate is blocked, the Operating Partner cannot assign work. When the plan needs the EA to do something (book a planning review, chase a vendor), it makes a peer request in Slack — colleague to colleague — in a channel the owner shares, so the owner is always privy to what was asked and why. It is a request the EA can decline, and the EA's own owner-approval gate on calendar/outbound is the real control point: the Operating Partner physically cannot cause an unapproved side effect by asking. When a hand-off is ambiguous or high-stakes, it escalates to the owner instead of guessing. This keeps it advisory and on the record — no shadow boss, no hidden agent-to-agent backchannel.

4.7 Interface & visibility

Pidgin is the control plane, not the workplace. The Operating Partner lives in the company's real spaces and shows up like an employee:

  • Everyday interface = Slack/email. The owner DMs it in Slack; it delivers the weekly digest and exception pings as Slack messages (or email); the plan is a real Drive spreadsheet. DMing it inside pidgin is a diagnostic/debug path only, not the workflow.
  • Broad read of shared, private write. It reads shared surfaces (the Slack channels it's a member of, shared Drive, the backlog) — none of which are privileged, they're shared by design. Candid planning happens in a private founder DM. It's blind to other agents' private journals and other owners' 1:1s.
  • Channel membership is a rollout dial. Its visibility is which real Slack channels it's invited into, exactly like an employee. Start lean (founder DM + #general + wherever the roadmap lives), widen as it's visibly missing signal.

4.8 Boundary with the Executive Assistant

The split is domain, not horizon. The Operating Partner spans strategy and tactics; what it does not own is the logistics stream.

Executive AssistantOperating Partner
OwnsThe inbox / calendar / logistics streamThe plan and the priorities
PostureReactiveProactive, opinionated
"Remind me to email Bob"Handles itNot its job
"Are we on track to ship, and what's first?"Not its jobHandles it

The Operating Partner decides what matters and in what order; the EA handles the mechanics of doing it. When the plan implies a logistics action, it hands it to the EA (4.6) rather than doing it — no dueling reminders, no touching the owner's calendar.

4.9 Identity & hire customization

  • display_name — a person's first name (John, Jack, Sarah), set at hire, like the EA recipe. Presented as a real employee.
  • role_title — operator-set at hire, recipe supplies a default (Operating Partner). Behavior is fixed by the recipe; the title is positioning the operator chooses — "Chief Strategy Officer," "Chief of Staff," "Founder's Associate," "StratOps." (Hire-time override is PID-17.)
  • Endeavor scope — captured at hire: what this Operating Partner coordinates.
  • Plan sheet — the specific Drive file it stewards; the only place the tracking-write privilege (4.5) applies.
  • Tone — expert, opinionated, proactive; concise; respects the owner's attention (the change-gated design is the tone made real); recommends rather than surveys, and says what it would do.

6Open questions

  • Tracking vs. canon cell mechanism. How does the Operating Partner (and the connector's approval gate) know which cells are ungated tracking vs. staged canon? Leaning a hire-time declaration of tracking ranges/columns (named ranges or a designated tab), so the split is data, not guesswork. Depends on PID-15.
  • Endeavor-scope representation. What is "endeavor scope" concretely — free-text mission + a set of Slack channels + the plan sheet, or something more structured that the change-probe can filter tasks/events by? Needs pinning before backlog signals (4.4 #1, #4) can be scoped to "this endeavor."
  • Sheet-edit as a change signal (4.4 #2). Detecting "someone edited the plan sheet" implies a Drive watcher — source 4 of the change-gated probe, which notes connector-side watcher push is not yet wired. Interim: reconcile the sheet on the daily floor wake rather than react to the edit in real time.
  • Deadline thresholds. Default N for "approaching" (e.g. within 3 days)? Per-endeavor override?
  • End of an endeavor. When an endeavor completes, what happens to its Operating Partner — archived, or repurposed? No lifecycle defined yet.
  • Multi-level reporting. An endeavor-scoped Operating Partner reporting up to an org-wide CoS reuses the "report transport when manager is another agent" piece the autonomy spec flags as not-yet-wired. Deferred; reports go to the human owner for now.
